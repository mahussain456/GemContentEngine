Part I · Essence
The brand in one sentence
If GEM only had time to say one thing, this is the thing.
Brand essence

"Six AI agents in one HTML file. One purchase, forever yours.
The opposite of the per-word, per-month, per-seat content stack."

The three things GEM is

Crafted, not assembled. A multi-agent pipeline with locked schemas — not a chat box dressed up as a tool. Each agent has one job, done well.

Owned, not rented. One HTML file. One purchase. Runs on your machine forever, even after the company that sold it disappears. (Including ours, hypothetically.)

Solo-first. Built by one person for one-person teams. The brand sounds like a thoughtful operator, not a marketing department.

Why the visual identity looks the way it looks

Forest green. Trust, calm, growth. Not the saturated tech-startup green — a deep, warm forest tone. We are not in a hurry; we are deliberate.

Warm cream, not white. Cream signals editorial craft over Silicon Valley sterility. Books, not dashboards.

Gold accent. Sparingly. Just enough to feel like a real product, not enough to feel like a casino.

Serif typography. Most software brands use sans-serif because everyone else does. Serif (Fraunces / Georgia) makes GEM legible as a brand the moment you see a screenshot.

The diamond mark. A simple hexagonal gem cut viewed from above, with a center spark. Faceted but uncomplicated — exactly like the product itself.

Sizing & clear space

  • Minimum size: Mark renders cleanly down to 16×16 px (favicon). Wordmark needs 64 px wide minimum.
  • Clear space: Always preserve at least the width of the inner spark (the center circle) of empty space around any version of the logo.
  • Preferred lockup by context: Horizontal for headers and email signatures · Stacked for avatars, app icons, splash screens · Mark only for favicons and tiny placements.

What not to do

✗
Don't recolor — the mark is forest green or sage-light only
✗
Don't stretch — the hexagon must stay regular
✗
Don't add drop shadows, glows, or 3D effects

Usage rules

  • The 60-30-10 rule. Warm Beige 60% (page) + Pine Green/Deep Forest 30% (text + structure) + Sage / Gold 10% (accents). When in doubt, use less green.
  • Never pure black on cream. Always Charcoal (#1A1A1A). Pure black is jarring on warm backgrounds.
  • Gold is a punctuation mark. One gold rule per spread. One gold badge per page. If you need two, you don't need any.
  • Sage on cream needs care. Sage (#A7BFAE) fails contrast on cream for text — use Pine Green (#1B4D3E) or darker for any reading copy. Sage is for soft surfaces and decorative type only.
  • Accessibility floor. Pine Green (#1B4D3E) on Warm Beige hits ~9.7:1 contrast — well above WCAG AAA. Stay there for body copy.
  • CSS tokens already exist. Use /assets/web-assets/gem-brand-tokens.css — drop-in variables: --gem-deep-forest, --gem-pine-green, --gem-sage, --gem-mist, --gem-warm-beige, --gem-stone, --gem-clay, --gem-charcoal, --gem-gold.
Part IV · Typography
Type system
Three families, three roles. Don't introduce a fourth.
SERIF · HEADLINES & BRANDPlayfair Display (primary) · EB Garamond (alt)Weights: 500, 600, 700
One idea. Every channel.
Six AI agents.

For hero headlines, section titles, and editorial moments. Playfair Display has the sharp, high-contrast strokes that read "premium publication" — pair with Inter for a classic editorial / modern hybrid. The wordmark in the official logo uses EB Garamond — they harmonize cleanly.

SANS · BODY & UIInter (web) · system-ui fallbackWeights: 400, 500, 600, 700
Body copy, navigation, buttons, form fields, dashboard text. Inter is the calm, neutral workhorse that lets the serif headlines do the talking. Reads cleanly at 13–15 px on screen.

For paragraphs, UI, buttons, captions. Default 13.5 px on web, line-height 1.65. Avoid going below 12 px for any reading copy.

MONO · LABELS & TECHNICALJetBrains Mono (web) · Consolas fallbackWeights: 500, 600
CONTENT ENGINE · THEGEMINFO.COM

Eyebrow labels (uppercase, letter-spaced .18em–.22em), code snippets, technical specs, footers. Mono is GEM's "indie technical builder" signal.

Pairing recipes that just work

  • Hero. Mono eyebrow (10px, .22em letter-spacing, Pine Green) → Playfair Display headline 48–72px (weight 500) → Inter lede 16–18px.
  • Section. Mono eyebrow (10px) → Playfair H2 (32–42px, weight 600) → Inter body (13.5–15px).
  • Card. Inter label (10px uppercase) → Playfair title (15–18px, weight 600) → Inter body (12.5–13px).
  • Pull quote. Playfair italic 18–22px on Mist background, with a Gold left border.

Part V · Voice
How GEM sounds
A thoughtful operator, not a marketing department. Sharp, no fluff, slightly contrarian.

The five rules

★ Do

Be specific. "$11K/month." "90 seconds." "Six agents." Numbers and names earn trust.

✗ Don't

Use generic SaaS-speak. "Solutions." "Empowering." "Seamless." If it could be on every other product page, cut it.

★ Do

Talk like a person. Contractions are fine. So are sentences that start with And or But.

✗ Don't

Be the marketing department. "Revolutionize your content workflow with cutting-edge AI" → instant trust loss.

★ Do

Show your work. "I built this in 6 weeks because…" Process beats polish.

✗ Don't

Pretend GEM is bigger than it is. No "our team is committed to…" It's one person. Lean into it.

★ Do

Be slightly contrarian. "Most AI tools rent you a chat box. GEM gives you a finished workflow."

✗ Don't

Be mean. Contrarian, not contemptuous. Don't trash competitors by name.

★ Do

End with a clear ask. Tell the reader what to do next. "Try the demo." "Buy at $9." "Reply with feedback."

✗ Don't

Bury the verb. "We're excited to share that GEM is now available…" → "GEM is live. Here's the link."
